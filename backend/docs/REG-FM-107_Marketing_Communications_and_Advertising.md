# Marketing Communications and Fund Advertising Rules (REG-FM-107)

## Article 1 — General Standard

All marketing communications relating to a Fund — including advertisements,
social media posts, factsheets, and websites — must be clear, fair, and not
misleading, and must be consistent with the disclosures in the Prospectus
and KIID.

## Article 2 — Performance Presentation

Where past performance is shown:

1. It must cover at least the preceding 5 calendar years, or since
   inception if shorter, and be net of all fees and charges;
2. It must be accompanied by the statement: "Past performance is not a
   reliable indicator of future results";
3. Cherry-picked time periods designed to present an unrepresentative
   picture of performance are prohibited;
4. Benchmark comparisons must use a benchmark consistent with the one
   disclosed in the Prospectus.

## Article 3 — Pre-Approval Requirement

Marketing communications intended for retail investors must be reviewed
and approved by the Fund Manager's compliance function prior to
publication. Records of approval, including the reviewer's name and date,
must be retained for 5 years.

## Article 4 — Target Market and Distribution Restrictions

A Fund Manager must define the target market for each Fund (retail,
professional, or both) and ensure marketing communications and
distribution channels are consistent with that target market. Complex
products (e.g. Funds using leverage above 100% of net assets, or with
side pockets activated) must include a prominent risk warning when
marketed to retail investors.

## Article 5 — Sustainability Claims

Any marketing communication referring to a Fund's sustainability or ESG
characteristics must be consistent with the disclosures required under
REG-FM-108 and must not overstate the proportion of sustainable
investments held by the Fund ("greenwashing"). Use of terms such as
"sustainable," "green," or "impact" in a Fund's name triggers the
disclosure obligations under Article 2 of REG-FM-108.

## Article 6 — Digital and Social Media Communications

Marketing via digital channels, including paid social media promotion,
must include the same risk warnings and balanced presentation required for
print media, adapted for the format. Influencer or affiliate marketing
arrangements must be disclosed as promotional content and are subject to
the same pre-approval requirement under Article 3.

## Article 7 — Corrective Action

Where the MFCA determines that a marketing communication is misleading, it
may require immediate withdrawal, a corrective communication to affected
recipients, and, for repeated or serious breaches, a public statement of
censure and an administrative fine of up to 1% of assets under management
attributable to the Fund.
