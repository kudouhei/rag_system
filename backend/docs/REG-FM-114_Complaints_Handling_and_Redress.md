# Client Complaints Handling and Redress (REG-FM-114)

## Article 1 — Complaints Handling Function

A Fund Manager and any distributor marketing its Funds must maintain a
complaints handling function, independent of the business unit generating
the complaint, empowered to investigate and resolve complaints and to
authorise redress up to a pre-agreed threshold.

## Article 2 — Acknowledgement and Resolution Timelines

Upon receipt of a complaint, the Fund Manager must:

1. Acknowledge receipt in writing within 3 business days;
2. Provide a final written response within 20 business days of receipt,
   or, where the complaint is complex, an interim update explaining the
   delay and a revised timeline not exceeding 40 business days in total;
3. Inform the complainant of their right to escalate to the Meridia
   Financial Ombudsman Service if unsatisfied with the outcome.

## Article 3 — Root Cause Analysis

Where a complaint reveals a systemic issue (e.g. a NAV calculation error
under REG-FM-102, or a misleading marketing communication under
REG-FM-107) affecting more than one investor, the Fund Manager must
conduct a root cause analysis, assess whether other investors are
similarly affected, and provide redress to all affected investors, not
only the complainant.

## Article 4 — Record Keeping and Reporting

All complaints, including those resolved informally, must be logged and
retained for 5 years. Fund Managers must submit a semi-annual Complaints
Report to the MFCA summarising complaint volumes, root causes, average
resolution time, and redress paid.

## Article 5 — Redress Calculation

Redress must place the investor, as far as possible, in the position they
would have been in had the failure not occurred, including compensation
for direct financial loss and, where appropriate, distress and
inconvenience. Redress payments related to NAV errors must follow the
compensation timeline in Article 3 of REG-FM-102.

## Article 6 — Prohibition on Discouraging Complaints

A Fund Manager or distributor must not discourage investors from making a
complaint, including through unreasonable administrative barriers, or
require an investor to waive their right to escalate to the Ombudsman
Service as a condition of receiving redress.

## Article 7 — Supervisory Consequences

A pattern of complaint mishandling, including repeated late responses or
inadequate root cause analysis, may result in an MFCA-mandated remediation
programme and, for serious or repeated failures, referral for enforcement
action.
